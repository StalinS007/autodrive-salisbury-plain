  • The reel itself: 125,532 views in three days. 56,444 on Facebook, 69,088 on Instagram.
  • 780 likes. 77 comments. 520 shares. 231 saves.
  • 643 people followed the account from it. 1,900 visited the profile.
  • Average watch time 28 seconds.

  • It built awareness and followers. The enquiries it brings will be the slow kind, from people who now recognise the name.

What one reel like this does over time

  • The 643 people it added are 55% of the whole Instagram audience.
  • Every reel from now on lands in front of 2.4× more people than in August, before anyone shares it.
  • The ordinary reel got 10× better because the account was posting.
  • The audience got 2.4× bigger because one of those reels travelled.
  • It took 37 reels in ten weeks to produce one that did this.
  • Post once a month and the odds of another are small. Post weekly and they are reasonable.
  • A big reel cannot be planned. The conditions for one can be kept up.

5. Is it reaching Adelaide? (the honest read)

  • Adelaide is the number one city in the audience on both platforms. 16.6% of Facebook followers, 20.3% of Instagram followers.
  • Next come Melbourne, Sydney and the overseas Malayali community.
  • Australia is 42% of the Facebook audience and 56% of the Instagram audience.
  • The everyday reels reach South Australia first. The 18 August BMW reel had SA as its top region. The 3 September van reel had SA in its top five.
  • The big reel went national and overseas. On Instagram 52% of its viewers were in Australia. On Facebook about 20%. The rest were in the UK, India, Canada and the Gulf.
  • Meta does not give Adelaide views per post. The closest measures are the follower split (Adelaide number one on both platforms) and the South Australia region on ordinary Facebook reels.
  • Many of its comments were in Malayalam and not local enquiries. I am not counting them as leads.

6. Why it has to be consistent (what the numbers say about the method)

  • The big reel is the exception. The proof of the method is the ordinary post.
  • An average post now reaches 430 people instead of 44. Ten times more.
  • That happened because the accounts post video every week, and the platforms reward it.
  • Reach, followers and Google calls all moved together after July.
  • None of them moved in the six months before, when posting was occasional and mostly photos.
  • Weekly reels are what local people see. The big ones grow the audience those weekly reels land on.
  • Both stop the moment posting stops. The platforms forget an account within weeks.
  • September's slower fortnight is what that looks like starting.
