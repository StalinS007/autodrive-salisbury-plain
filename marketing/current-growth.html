The 8 September reel, "Are you looking for a family car?" 125,532 views in three days (56,444 on Facebook, 69,088 on Instagram). 780 likes, 77 comments, 520 shares, 231 saves. 643 people followed the account from that one reel, and 1,900 visited the profile. Average watch time 28 seconds. 98.7% of the watch time came from Instagram and Facebook recommending it to people who do not follow us. That is the platform choosing to show AutoDrive to strangers because the video held their attention.

4. Is it reaching Adelaide? (the honest read)

  • Adelaide is the number one city in the audience on both platforms: 16.6% of Facebook followers and 20.3% of Instagram followers, ahead of Melbourne, Sydney and the overseas Malayali community. Australia is 42% of the Facebook audience and 56% of the Instagram audience.
  • The everyday reels reach South Australia first. The 18 August BMW reel had South Australia as its top region. The 3 September van reel had South Australia in its top five. These are the videos that bring local people to the page.
  • The viral reel went national and overseas. About 20% of its Facebook viewers were in Australia and the rest were in the UK, India, Canada and the Gulf. It built the brand and the follower base; on its own it does not fill the workshop. Many of its comments were in Malayalam and not local enquiries, and I am not counting them as leads.
  • What did come through: a customer called the workshop after seeing the videos, the profile calls tripled on Google, and word of mouth is now landing on a page that looks busy and trusted, with 26 five-star reviews. Someone who is referred to AutoDrive and looks it up now sees a business that is active every week.
  • The audience is about 90% men aged 25 to 44, which is the buyer for used family cars and servicing.

5. Why it has to be consistent (what the numbers say about the method)

The big reel is the exception. The proof of the method is the ordinary post: an average post now reaches 430 people instead of 44, ten times more, because the accounts post video every week and the platforms reward that. Reach, followers and Google calls all moved together after July, and none of them moved in the six months before, when posting was occasional and mostly photos.

  • Weekly reels are what local people see. The viral ones grow the audience those weekly reels land on. Both stop the moment posting stops, and the platforms forget an account within weeks.
  • There is already filmed footage waiting to be cut, so the next two weeks of content do not depend on new shoots.
