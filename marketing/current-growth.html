Current growth: AutoDrive Salisbury Plain

Prepared by Stalin for Jitty George · 12 September 2026 · Numbers read from Google Business Profile, Meta Business Suite, Instagram and Google Analytics on 11–12 September

In the first week of July I put the case to Jitty that AutoDrive needed to be posting video regularly, and set the direction for it: short reels, one topic each, every week, all pointing back to the phone and the website. This document shows what has changed since then on the places customers find the business: Google, Facebook, Instagram and the website. The big reel of 8 September is shown separately from everything else, so the everyday result and the exceptional one can each be seen for what they are. Where a number is small or only partly proven, I say so.

The reel itself: 125,532 views in three days (56,444 Facebook, 69,088 Instagram). 780 likes, 77 comments, 520 shares, 231 saves. 643 people followed the account from it and 1,900 visited the profile. Average watch time 28 seconds. 98.7% of the watch time came from Instagram and Facebook recommending it to people who do not follow us: the platforms chose to show AutoDrive to strangers because the video held their attention. On the website it produced a two-day spike (25 and 31 visitors on 8 and 9 September, the second-busiest day since tracking began) that mostly landed on the home page. It built awareness and followers; the enquiries it will bring are the slow kind, from people who now recognise the name.

What one reel like this does over time. The 643 people it added are 55% of the whole Instagram audience; every reel posted from now on lands in front of 2.4 times more people than in August, before anyone shares it. That is how consistent exposure compounds: the ordinary reel got 10× better because the account was posting, and the audience it reaches got 2.4× bigger because one of those reels travelled. It took 37 reels in ten weeks to produce one that did this. Post one a month and the odds of another are small; post weekly and they are reasonable. A big reel cannot be planned, but the conditions for one can be kept up.

5. Is it reaching Adelaide? (the honest read)

  • Adelaide is the number one city in the audience on both platforms: 16.6% of Facebook followers and 20.3% of Instagram followers, ahead of Melbourne, Sydney and the overseas Malayali community. Australia is 42% of the Facebook audience and 56% of the Instagram audience.
  • The everyday reels reach South Australia first. The 18 August BMW reel had South Australia as its top region. The 3 September van reel had South Australia in its top five. These are the videos that bring local people to the page.
  • The big reel went national and overseas. About 20% of its Facebook viewers were in Australia and the rest were in the UK, India, Canada and the Gulf. Many of its comments were in Malayalam and not local enquiries, and I am not counting them as leads.

6. Why it has to be consistent (what the numbers say about the method)

The big reel is the exception. The proof of the method is the ordinary post: an average post now reaches 430 people instead of 44, ten times more, because the accounts post video every week and the platforms reward that. Reach, followers and Google calls all moved together after July, and none of them moved in the six months before, when posting was occasional and mostly photos.

  • Weekly reels are what local people see. The big ones grow the audience those weekly reels land on. Both stop the moment posting stops, and the platforms forget an account within weeks. September's slower fortnight is what that looks like starting.
  • There is already filmed footage waiting to be cut, so the next two weeks of content do not depend on new shoots.
