What this brings in

Through consistent posting — sixteen videos a month, every week, all year — the videos reach about 7,000 people a month, and 700–1,400 of them are in South Australia, on top of the Adelaide followers who see every post. We don't need many of them. We need a handful.

Where each number comes from

  • 430 people — what an ordinary post reaches. Average reach per post from 6 July to 10 September, with the 8 September family-car reel taken out (with it in, the average is 1,211). Before July the same figure was 44. Seen in: Meta Business Suite → Insights → Content, sorted by reach, 1 Jan – 10 Sep 2026. Captured 11 Sep.
  • 16 videos a month — the posting rate in this plan (4 a week). Since July the account has actually been posting more than that (6.5 posts a week, 37 reels in 67 days), so 16 is the floor, not a stretch. Seen in: same Content list, counted by date.
  • ~7,000 people a month = 16 × 430 = 6,880. That's posts only, at the ordinary average. Cross-check: total reach in the 67 days after the pivot, with the viral reel removed, was about 47,000 across both platforms — roughly 21,000 a month. We're using the smaller number on purpose. Seen in: Insights → Results, daily reach summed: Instagram 92,452 and Facebook 58,588, less the viral reel's 56,733 + 46,899.
  • 10–20% in South Australia — so 700–1,400 of the 7,000. Read off two ordinary reels' viewer breakdowns: 18 Aug BMW reel, 32 of 157 viewers in SA (20%); 3 Sep van reel, 54 of 574 (9%). SA was the #1 region on the first and top-5 on the second. Seen in: Business Suite → Content → Posts & reels → open the post → viewer region (Facebook shows region for 3-second viewers).
  • ~300 Adelaide followers who see every post. Adelaide is the #1 city for followers on both platforms — 20.3% of 1,162 Instagram followers (≈ 236) and 16.6% of 459 Facebook followers (≈ 76). Seen in: Insights → Audience → top cities (lifetime, no date filter). Follower counts captured 11 Sep.

What it takes

Everything in this plan, done properly, is five separate jobs. Bought the normal way, each one is a different person to find, brief, and chase.

StepWhat it is
1Video direction — scripts, hooks and thumbnails for every video; planning the shoot; and the back-and-forth correcting the Digital Volcano team's edits until they're right.
  • Video production — up to 4 a week, filmed with me
  • I'll film and edit some myself as well — to test what works and try new things for you. The plan is to automate the editing over time so the videos get made without needing anyone.
2Social management — someone posts, captions, replies, runs the groups
3Google Business Profile — photos, posts, reviews, Q&A
4Website upkeep — listings, prices, booking flow
5Reporting and strategy — someone reads the numbers and tells you what to do next
6Growth strategy — bringing in new technology as it arrives (automation, AI, better booking tools) and keeping you on top of what's changing, so the business stays ahead instead of catching up

How the business grows

The wheel every service business turns, with AutoDrive's names on it. Each step feeds the next; the videos get it moving.

1Videos bring new customers. The reels put the workshop in front of people with a problem — the sales step, done in public, sixteen times a month.
2Enough mechanics for the work. Critical mass: the right number of hands on the floor for the demand the videos create — bookings taken, not turned away.
3Reliable suppliers & partners. Parts, paint, tyres, editors — steady, so the work never waits on someone else.
4Great service. The part Jitty already owns — 5.0 stars from 26 reviews.
5They come back & refer. A serviced car returns twice a year; a happy customer brings the next one. Reviews asked for after every job.
6Growth, reinvested. More cars through the door pays for the next turn — more videos, more mechanics, better tools.
