Reel Playbook

The idea in one line: every reel names ONE problem a driver is having right now, shows you fixing it, and sends them to the link in bio — where the website turns them into a WhatsApp booking with their car's details already filled in. How to film it, script by script.

Prepared by Stalin · For Jitty · August 2026

The formula

Anatomy of a 30-second reel

  • 0–2 sHook — say the symptom to camera. First words name the problem the viewer has: “ABS light flashing while you drive?” Nothing else opens the video.
  • 3–10 sWhat it means. One or two sentences — what's probably wrong and why leaving it costs more later. Plain words, no jargon.
  • 10–25 sShow the fix. Real footage: the car on the hoist, the worn part next to the new one, the scanner reading. Your workshop is the proof.
  • 25–30 sThe same CTA, every time. Word for word — it's the last beat of every script card below.
One video = ONE symptom. Never list five problems in one reel — the person with the ABS light needs to feel like this video was made for them.

A different format

The comparison reel

The five scripts above sell repairs. This one sells services — and it works differently. Instead of naming a problem, it answers the question every customer is quietly asking: “am I being upsold?”

Answer it honestly, including when the cheap option is the right one. A comparison reel that always lands on “buy the expensive one” reads as a sales pitch and kills trust. One that says “most cars don't need this” is the reason people book with you instead of the shop down the road.

Two versions — film them as separate reels, one idea per video, same as every other script.

Version A — “Is expensive engine oil actually worth it?”
  • 0–2 s · HookTwo oil bottles held up side by side, one in each hand. Hook spoken and on screen as text.
  • 3–10 s · The differenceBoth protect your engine. The better one stays thinner when it's cold and doesn't break down as fast when it's hot — and most engine wear happens in the first ten seconds after a cold start.
  • 10–25 s · Show itThe proof shot: drained oil from a car run on cheap oil next to oil drained at the same kilometres on full synthetic — black sludge vs still-amber. Two jars on the bench, labelled. If you don't have that pair yet, start saving drain samples now — this is the whole video.
  • 25–30 s · Verdict + CTAShort trips around Adelaide in a normal car? The standard oil is fine — don't let anyone tell you otherwise. Turbo, diesel, towing, or a car you want to keep ten years? Pay the extra. Not sure which is yours — link in bio, tell us your car, we'll tell you straight.
Sells: trust first, servicing second — the honesty IS the pitch
Version B — “$149 or $329 service? Here's the actual difference.”
  • 0–2 s · HookWide shot of the bench with the parts already laid out in three groups. Hook spoken and on screen as text.
  • 3–10 s · Lay it outWalk the bench and name each group as you point: Basic — oil, filter, safety check. Standard — all that plus air filter, battery test, brakes, fault scan, road test. Premium — plus cabin filter, fuel system treatment and a written report.
  • 10–25 s · Show itClose on the physical parts — this is why the format works: the customer SEES what their money buys instead of reading a list. Hold up the dirty cabin filter that came out of a real car; that one shot sells Premium on its own.
  • 25–30 s · Verdict + CTAMost cars doing normal Adelaide kilometres want the Standard. Go Premium if it's high-k, tows, or you're about to road-trip it. Prices and booking in bio.
Sells: the $229 Standard tier — named as the default choice, which is where most bookings should land

Caption tweak for these two: the first line is still a question the customer is asking — “Is expensive oil worth it?” or “$149 or $329 service?” — not a list of what you offer. Everything else in the caption rules below stays the same.

The caption

Example of a finished caption

This exact caption is on the pinned reel on Instagram — open it and copy the shape every time:

Engine light on? Everyone fears the worst. This Nissan needed one small part instead of four. We test first, so you only pay for what is actually broken. Adelaide mechanic in Salisbury Plain. Prices and booking in bio 👆 #adelaidemechanic #salisburyplain #checkenginelight #carservice #adelaide
  • Under 30 words before the hashtags. Short captions perform better. If it feels too short, it is right.
  • The first line is the customer's problem, not our slogan. “Engine light on?” works. “Best service in town!” does not.
  • Write “Adelaide” somewhere in the text every time — not just in the hashtags. This is how local people find us.
  • Normal letters only. No “fancy bold” Instagram fonts — Instagram search cannot read them.
  • Maximum 5 hashtags, each with the # symbol. At least two local ones: #adelaidemechanic #salisburyplain.

When sharing the same reel to the Facebook community groups, swap “bio” for the special group link Stalin gave you — it lets us see exactly which group sends customers.
