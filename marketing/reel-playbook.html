Reel Playbook

The idea in one line: every reel names ONE problem a driver is having right now, shows you fixing it, and sends them to the link in bio — where the website turns them into a WhatsApp booking with their car's details already filled in. How to film it, script by script.

Prepared by Stalin · For Jitty · August 2026

The formula

Anatomy of a 30-second reel

  • 0–2 sHook — say the symptom to camera. First words name the problem the viewer has: “ABS light flashing while you drive?” Nothing else opens the video.
  • 3–10 sWhat it means. One or two sentences — what's probably wrong and why leaving it costs more later. Plain words, no jargon.
  • 10–25 sShow the fix. Real footage: the car on the hoist, the worn part next to the new one, the scanner reading. Your workshop is the proof.
  • 25–30 sThe same CTA, every time. Word for word — see the black box below.
One video = ONE symptom. Never list five problems in one reel — the person with the ABS light needs to feel like this video was made for them.
Ready to shoot

Ten scripts — film straight off this list

“ABS light flashing while you drive?”
  • 0–2 s · HookOpen tight on the flashing ABS light. Say the hook to camera — and put it on screen as text. Nothing else opens the video.
  • 3–10 s · What it meansThat light means the system that stops you skidding is off duty. Usually a sensor — cheap to check, dangerous to ignore in the wet.
  • 10–25 s · Show the fixThe scanner pulling the fault code; the wheel sensor coming off the hub; the light gone on the road test.
  • 25–30 s · CTAIf that's your car — link in bio, tap ‘Issues with my car’, tell us what's wrong, and we'll sort it.
Sells: diagnostics — a real customer already booked from this exact problem

The close

Same CTA, word for word, every reel

“If that's your car — link in bio, tap ‘Issues with my car’, tell us what's wrong, and we'll sort it.”

Say it to camera AND put it as text on screen for the last 3 seconds. Repetition is the point — followers should be able to finish the sentence for you. That's when it's working.
