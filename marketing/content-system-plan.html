The AutoDrive Content System

What this is: a weekly system that keeps sixteen videos a month moving — filmed, scripted, edited, posted at the right time, tracked — so you get your evenings back, and the audience keeps growing instead of spiking and going quiet.

Prepared by Stalin · For Jitty · September 2026

1Sixteen videos a month, filmed for the month

  • Film 8 videos a fortnight or 4 a week — and slowly work up to 5–6 quality videos a week, not just more.
  • Spread across the business: 1–2 videos per sector — servicing, problems and repairs, used cars, detailing, paint and panel.
  • Hook and script every one. Pain-and-problem videos, each one laid out in advance. I only need you for one day a week or fortnight — the rest is mine.
  • Thumbnails on everything. Right now the videos go up without one — they look wishy-washy and don't call out to the customer. A thumbnail that names the problem stops the scroll.
  • DV posts; they don't manage. They write captions with AI and press post. People can tell — they don't engage with fake-sounding stuff. Social media has to be watched and adjusted based on what's performing. That's the part that's been missing.

How the week runs

I personally check every video — edit it myself if it needs it, or send it back to the DV team and work with them until it's right. Every Friday by 8 pm I send you the batch for the coming week (4 videos, or 8 for the fortnight) for a yes. I'll call DV and set the system up with them myself.

The goal
  • You stop chasing them. No more messages back and forth during the week — one check on a Friday. One a fortnight once we're 8 ahead.
  • Less after-hours work and bad communication. You focus on the workshop.
  • A pipeline that's always ahead — and an audience that keeps growing. This is the compound effect: every week builds on the last instead of starting again.
  • More data, better videos. I tweak the scripts as the numbers come in, to hit the people we actually want — Adelaide, our customers.
  • Fortnightly check-in. I show you how we performed. You tell me anything that came from our online presence — an enquiry, a sale, a booking, someone who said "I saw your video." Then we double down on what worked.

The reach, honestly stated. Since the videos changed in July, far more people see them — but most of the biggest number came from one reel, so here it is both ways.

  • Instagram reach, 6 July – 10 September (67 days): 92,452 — against 3,084 in the six months before (1 January – 5 July). The 8 September family-car reel is more than half of that (~57,000). Without it: about 35,000 — still eleven times the previous six months, in a third of the time. Source: Meta Business Suite → Insights → Results, daily reach summed.
  • Facebook reach, same window: 58,588 against 2,869 before. Same source.
  • An ordinary post reaches about 430 people (viral reel excluded), against 44 before. Source: Meta Business Suite → Insights → Content, average reach per post, 1 Jan – 10 Sep.
  • How much of that is local: on the everyday reels South Australia is the #1 or top-5 region — but it's 10–20% of viewers, not all of them. 18 Aug BMW reel: 32 of 157 viewers in SA. 3 Sep van reel: 54 of 574. On top of that, Adelaide is the #1 city among followers (20% on Instagram, 17% on Facebook), and followers see every post. Source: per-post audience breakdown in Business Suite; Insights → Audience.
  • Followers: 1,162 on Instagram, 459 on Facebook — up from roughly 350 and 190 in early July. Source: Insights → Audience; profile on 7 Sep showed 485 before the viral reel.

So the funnel, using the ordinary numbers, not the viral one:

  • 16 posts a month × ~430 people = about 7,000 people a month seeing the videos
  • 10–20% of them in South Australia = 700–1,400 local people a month, plus ~300 Adelaide followers who see everything
  • We need eleven of them to become customers — about 1 in 100. The rest is upside.

The maths, line by line

  • Servicing: average service $300–500. Five people → 5 × $300 = $1,500, up to 5 × $500 = $2,500.
  • Detailing: $200–700 a job. Three people → 3 × $200 = $600, up to 3 × $700 = $2,100.
  • Used cars: a $10,000–20,000 car carries $1,500–3,000 margin. One car → $1,500–3,000.
  • Paint & panel: $800–3,000 a job (small scuff repairs $300–600). Two jobs → 2 × $800 = $1,600, up to 2 × $3,000 = $6,000.
  • Together, low end: $1,500 + $600 + $1,500 + $1,600 = $5,200 a month.
  • Together, high end: $2,500 + $2,100 + $3,000 + $6,000 = $13,600 a month.
  • Over a year: 11 customers × 12 months = 132 new customers. The job prices are yours to correct — put your real numbers in and the shape of it doesn't change.
The long-term value
  • Those eleven don't leave. A service customer at $400, twice a year, for five years = $4,000. Fifty of them = $200,000 sitting in the customer base, coming back on their own.
  • The videos keep working after they're posted. A "brakes squealing?" reel from October is still being found in March. Every week adds to the library; nothing falls off.
  • Word of mouth multiplies it. A lot of this will arrive as referrals — someone saw the video, told their brother. That won't show up in any dashboard, and it's probably the bigger number.
  • It will be slow at first. Months one to three build the base. Then it compounds — the same effort brings more each month, because last month's work is still paying.

6Where this goes

The videos are the start, not the point. The point is a brand — the name people in the community say first when someone asks "who do you trust with your car?" A brand is built one way only: consistent value, in public, for a long time. That's what the system is for. And once it exists, it can do things a workshop on its own never could.

NowAdelaide — the trusted name in the community. South Australia is already the top region on the everyday reels, and Adelaide the #1 city among followers. Every week we add to that until AutoDrive is the default for the Indian and migrant community here — servicing, cars, detailing, panel. The wider Adelaide market follows, with its own approach, once the community brand is solid.
NextInterstate is already watching. The family-car video's viewers were 52% Australia, 11% UK, 9% India, 8% New Zealand, 5% Canada — and inside Australia, South Australia wasn't even in its top ten regions. The views were interstate and diaspora. (Source: reel insights, Audience tab.) Those people have family in Sydney and Melbourne who trust the same name. First they ring for advice. Then we give them somewhere to go: mechanics in those cities that AutoDrive has vetted and vouches for, so a customer who trusts us is looked after wherever they are.
ThenIf it keeps growing — branches. Opening in another state is hard when nobody knows you. It's much easier when the audience is already there before the doors open. The brand goes first; the workshop follows it.
It's been done

Royalty Auto Service — a father-and-son independent workshop in a small town in Georgia, USA — spent two years posting real repairs, explained to camera, nothing fancy. Now customers drive from other states, some staying overnight in a hotel, to get their car fixed at "the shop from YouTube". They're opening a third location. Their own advice: simple topics work best, and real beats polished every time. That's exactly what Jitty already does on camera — the only thing they had that we don't yet is the consistency.

The goal, in one line: be the most consistent provider of value online for our community — so that they believe us first, and everything else becomes possible from there.
