Why it feels so hard

Buying a car shouldn't take weeks of stress

Hundreds of listings. Sellers who go quiet. History checks, inspections, test drives, paperwork, transfer fees. And the nagging worry that you'll pay too much for a car with a problem nobody told you about.

Most people go about it backwards: they scroll the ads first and fall for a car before they know what they actually need or what it will really cost. This strategy flips that around. Follow the six steps in order and you'll spend less time searching and far less money fixing.

01

Set your real budget

The price on the ad is not what the car costs you. Work out the total before you look, so you never stretch for a car you can't comfortably run.

02

Pick the car, not the ad

Decide what you need first. Then only look at listings that fit. Answer these honestly:

  • Who rides in it? Just you, a partner, kids in car seats, older parents? This decides hatch, sedan, SUV or 7-seater.
  • How far do you drive? Long daily commutes favour a frugal, reliable car. Short city trips favour something small and easy to park.
  • Safety: look up the model's ANCAP rating and check it has the airbags and driver aids you want.
  • Running costs: fuel use, service costs, tyre sizes and parts prices vary a lot between brands.
  • Reliability: a proven model with a full service history beats a flashier car with gaps in its past.
Write down 2 or 3 models that fit, your maximum kilometres and your oldest acceptable year. Now you're searching with a filter, not a wish.
03

Shortlist and spot red flags

Compare the same model across several listings so you know the fair price. Then walk away from any of these:

  • Price well under the others for the same year and kilometres. There's usually a reason.
  • Seller won't give the VIN or the rego number before you visit.
  • Wants to meet in a car park instead of at their home or business.
  • No service history, or the story changes when you ask about it.
  • Pressure to pay a deposit before you've seen the car, or "other buyers are coming tonight".
  • Name on the rego papers doesn't match the seller's ID.

04

Check the history before you fall in love

Ten minutes of checks can save you thousands. Do these before you even book a test drive.

  • PPSR search at ppsr.gov.au with the VIN. It costs a couple of dollars and shows if there's money owing on the car, if it's been written off, or if it's reported stolen. If finance is owing, it can stay with the car, not the seller.
  • Service history: ask for the logbook or receipts. Look for regular services and check the kilometres climb steadily from one stamp to the next.
  • VIN match: the VIN on the car must match the rego papers and your PPSR search.
  • Recalls: search the model on the government recalls website and ask if any recall work has been done.
05

Inspect and test drive properly

Ask the seller not to start the car before you arrive. A cold start tells you the most.

  • Starts cleanly from cold, no rattles or knocking
  • No warning lights left on after starting
  • No blue or thick white smoke from the exhaust
  • Smooth gear changes, no slipping or clunks
  • Brakes stop straight with no shudder or squeal
  • Steering doesn't pull to one side on a straight road
  • Air con blows cold, every window and lock works
  • Tyres worn evenly, with plenty of tread
  • Panel gaps and paint colour match all round
  • No damp carpet, musty smell or oil under the car
South Australia doesn't require a roadworthy certificate for most private sales, so a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic is your protection. It costs far less than one surprise repair.

06

Negotiate and transfer safely

You've done the work. Now make sure you pay a fair price and the car is legally yours.

Negotiate with facts, not feelings

  • Use what you found: the prices of similar cars, tyres or brakes that are due, a service that's overdue.
  • Make one fair offer and be ready to walk away. There is always another car.
  • Never let a deadline or "someone else is interested" rush you past your checks.

Pay safely

  • Pay only once the checks are clear and you're ready to collect the car.
  • Use a bank transfer you can trace. Avoid carrying large amounts of cash.
  • Get a signed receipt with the date, price, VIN, rego number and both names.

Transfer it into your name

  • Buyer and seller both complete their parts of the transfer, then lodge it with Service SA (online or at a centre) and pay the stamp duty.
  • Do it straight away. There's a time limit and late fees apply if you leave it.
  • Sort your insurance before you drive the car home.
Private sale or licensed dealer? A private sale can be cheaper, but you take on all the risk. Buying from a licensed dealer in SA usually comes with a statutory warranty depending on the car's age and kilometres, and the dealer must give you a clear title.
