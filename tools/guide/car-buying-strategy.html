The problem

Buying a car is hard

Too many ads. Sellers who don't reply. Checks, test drives and forms. And the fear of paying too much for a bad car.

Most people look at ads first. That's the mistake. Do these 6 steps in order. You'll save time and money.

01

Know your budget

The price on the ad is not the full cost. Add these up first.

The carThe price you pay the seller.
Stamp dutyA government fee when the car goes into your name. In SA it's about 4% of the price.
RegoHow many months are left? If it runs out soon, you pay to renew it.
InsuranceGet a quote before you buy. Some cars cost a lot more to insure.
Mechanic checkPay a mechanic to check the car before you buy.
Spare moneyKeep 5 to 10% aside. You may need a service, tyres or brakes soon.
Your real budgetYour total money, minus all of the above. That's what you can spend on the car.
Have your money ready before you look. Good cars sell fast. If your money isn't ready, someone else gets the car.

02

Know what car you need

Decide this before you look at any ads.

  • Who's in the car? Just you? Kids? Parents? That tells you hatch, sedan, SUV or 7-seater.
  • How far do you drive? Long drives every day? Get a car that uses little fuel. Mostly city? Get a small car that's easy to park.
  • Is it safe? Look up the car's ANCAP star rating. 5 stars is best.
  • Is it cheap to run? Fuel, services, tyres and parts cost more on some cars.
  • Does it break down? Pick a model known for lasting. A full service history is a must.
Write down 2 or 3 car models, the most kilometres you'll accept and the oldest year you'll accept. Only look at ads that match.
03

Spot the bad ads

Look at the same car in a few ads so you know the normal price. Walk away if:

  • The price is way lower than the rest. There's a reason.
  • The seller won't give you the VIN or rego number.
  • They want to meet in a car park, not at their home or shop.
  • There's no service book, or their story keeps changing.
  • They want a deposit before you see the car, or say "someone else is coming tonight".
  • The name on the rego papers is not the seller's name.

04

Check the car's past

Do this before you go and see the car. It takes 10 minutes.

  • Do a PPSR check at ppsr.gov.au using the VIN. It costs about $2. It tells you if money is owed on the car, if it was written off, or if it's stolen. If money is owed, you could lose the car.
  • Look at the service book. It should have regular stamps. The kilometres should go up each time.
  • Match the VIN. The VIN on the car must match the rego papers and the PPSR check.
  • Check for recalls. Search the car on the government recalls website.
A PPSR listing is not always bad. Some cars were damaged, then fixed properly. These can be great value, often much cheaper. The key is to buy it through a mechanic you trust, who checked the repair. Ask us about one on WhatsApp.
05

Look and drive

Ask the seller not to start the car before you get there. A cold engine shows problems.

  • Starts easily, no knocking sounds
  • No warning lights stay on
  • No blue or thick white smoke
  • Gears change smoothly
  • Brakes stop straight, no shaking
  • Car drives straight, doesn't pull
  • Air con is cold, all windows work
  • Tyres have good tread, worn evenly
  • Paint colour matches on every panel
  • No wet carpet, bad smell or oil leaks
In SA, most private sellers don't need a roadworthy certificate. So get a mechanic to check the car before you pay. It costs less than one surprise repair.

06

Pay and sign

Pay a fair price. Make sure the car is really yours.

Get a fair price

  • Use what you found. Other ads with lower prices. Tyres or brakes that need doing. A service that's late.
  • Make one fair offer. Be ready to walk away. There's always another car.
  • Don't let anyone rush you.

Pay safely

  • Only pay when all the checks are done and you're taking the car.
  • Pay by bank transfer. Don't carry lots of cash.
  • Get a signed receipt. It needs the date, price, VIN, rego number and both names.

Put the car in your name

  • You and the seller fill in the transfer form. Lodge it with Service SA, online or in person, and pay the stamp duty.
  • Do it straight away. If you're late, you pay a fine.
  • Get insurance before you drive the car home.
Private seller or dealer? A private seller can be cheaper, but if something goes wrong, it's on you. A licensed dealer in SA usually has to give you a warranty, depending on the car's age and kilometres.
